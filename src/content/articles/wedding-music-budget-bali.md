Live wedding music doesn't have a single price you can look up and compare across vendors, which makes it one of the harder line items to budget for early in planning. The actual cost depends on a handful of specific factors rather than a flat rate per hour, so instead of chasing a number before you have the details that determine it, it helps to understand what changes the cost and what to ask before you request a quote.

## What Actually Changes the Cost of Live Wedding Music?

Four factors do most of the work: how many musicians are in the formation, how much of the day you want live music for, how long each set runs, and venue logistics. A Solo pianist covering just the ceremony is a different scope than a Large Ensemble covering ceremony, cocktail hour, and dinner — and that's before venue location enters the picture. This is why we quote per couple rather than publishing a flat rate: two weddings booking the same formation can still land at different costs depending on scope.

## How Does Ensemble Size Affect the Budget?

More musicians generally means a higher cost, simply because more people are performing and traveling to your venue. But bigger isn't automatically better for your day — an intimate garden ceremony for 30 guests doesn't need the same formation as a beachfront wedding for 200, and matching size to your actual venue and guest count is a bigger factor in the right fit than chasing the largest option available. See [Packages](/packages/) for how each formation — Solo, Duo, Trio, String Quartet, and Large Ensemble — scales, and what each one suits.

## Does Live Music Cost More Than a DJ or a Band?

This comparison usually isn't apples to apples, because a DJ, a band, and a chamber ensemble tend to cover different parts of the day rather than compete for the same hours. A DJ or band is typically booked for reception dancing; a chamber ensemble covers the ceremony, prelude, and often cocktail hour. Rather than asking which is cheaper in the abstract, it's more useful to compare what each one costs for the specific hours and moments it's covering. Our [wedding band vs. DJ vs. chamber ensemble guide](/journal/wedding-band-vs-dj/) breaks down where each fits.

## Should You Set a Music Budget Before or After Booking Your Venue?

After you have at least a shortlist, if you can manage it. Your venue affects the logistics behind a quote — how far it is from where musicians are based, whether it's indoor or outdoor, what time your ceremony starts — so a number given before the venue is confirmed is necessarily a rough range, not a firm figure. It's reasonable to hold a provisional budget earlier in planning; just treat it as provisional until the venue is set. If you're still comparing venues, our guide to [choosing a venue for live music](/journal/wedding-venue-live-music-bali/) covers what to check before you commit.

## What Should You Ask a Vendor Before You Request a Quote?

Come with a few details ready, and you'll get a more accurate number back: your date, your venue (or shortlist), your ceremony start time, which moments you want covered, and your approximate guest count. It's also worth asking upfront whether the venue has any sound restrictions or power limitations, since that can affect formation size or setup — details covered in our guide above. The more specific you can be, the less back-and-forth it takes to land on an accurate quote.

## When Should You Request a Custom Quote?

Once your date and venue are set, even if other details are still in motion. Popular dates in Bali can book out 6 to 12 months ahead, especially during peak wedding season, so reaching out early secures your date while leaving room to confirm the exact formation and moments covered later. There's no need to have every detail finalized before you ask — a rough shape of your day is enough to start.
