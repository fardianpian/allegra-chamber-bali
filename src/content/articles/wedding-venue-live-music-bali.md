Most venue checklists cover the view, the guest count, and the backup plan for rain — and stop there. If live music matters to you, a few more questions belong on that list before you sign anything: whether the venue allows amplified sound, what time music needs to stop, and whether there's power and space for musicians to set up. None of this is complicated, but it's easy to miss when you're deciding on a venue for its view and atmosphere rather than its sound. Here's what to check, and when to check it.

## What Should You Ask About Live Music Before You Book a Venue?

Three things cover most of what actually affects your music: whether the venue allows live and amplified sound for the parts of the day you want covered, what time music needs to stop, and whether there's power and physical space for musicians near the ceremony spot. Ask all three during the same call or site visit where you're evaluating the venue — not after you've already signed a contract. Venues that host weddings regularly can usually answer immediately; if a venue hesitates or doesn't know, that's worth noting too.

## Does the Venue Allow Live or Amplified Music?

Most Bali ceremony venues welcome live acoustic music without any friction — piano and strings are common enough that many staff already know how to accommodate them. Amplification is where it gets more specific. A beachfront venue near residential areas, or a property with close neighbors, may cap volume or restrict amplified sound to certain hours; a private estate or resort ballroom typically has more room to work with. Don't assume either way — ask the venue directly, and if live music is central to your ceremony plan, get the answer in writing.

## What Time Do Bali Venues Usually Require Music to Stop?

There's no single curfew that applies across the island — each venue sets its own cutoff, usually shaped by its neighbors, location, and its own house policy. This tends to matter most for an evening reception with dancing rather than a daytime ceremony, but it's worth confirming for either. Ask the venue for their exact cutoff time, get it in writing, and build it into your day's timeline rather than assuming standard practice — "standard" varies more than couples expect.

## Indoor, Outdoor, or Semi-Open — How Does the Venue Change the Sound?

Outdoor live music doesn't sound worse than indoor — it just behaves differently, and the venue's structure is what changes that.

| Setting            | What Changes                                | What We Adjust                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chapel or ballroom | Walls contain and reflect sound naturally   | Minimal — the room does most of the work                 |
| Garden             | Foliage absorbs sound; ambient garden noise | Formation size and seating distance                      |
| Cliffside or beach | Open air, wind, waves                       | Placement, formation size, sometimes light amplification |

None of these rule live music out — they change how we set up for it. Tell us your venue type when you inquire and we'll confirm the right formation and placement for it.

## Does the Venue Have Power and Space for Musicians?

If your formation includes a digital piano (the standard choice for most outdoor Bali ceremonies — see our [beach](/journal/beach-wedding-music-bali/) and [garden](/journal/garden-wedding-ceremony-music-bali/) venue guides for why) or any light amplification, musicians need a power source within reach of where they'll set up. They also need enough physical space near the ceremony spot — not just wherever guest chairs end — and shade or cover if the setup will sit in direct sun for an extended soundcheck and performance. Walk the exact ceremony location with these needs in mind, not only where guests will be seated.

## When Should You Confirm Music Logistics With the Venue?

As early as you can — ideally during the same visit or call where you're deciding on the venue itself. A workable order: shortlist venues, confirm their live music policy and power/space situation, then finalize your booking with that information factored in. If you've already booked your venue, confirm these details next, before you lock in your musicians, so there are no surprises on the day.
