If your wedding is inside a resort ballroom rather than on a cliffside lawn, it's fair to wonder whether live chamber music works the same way indoors. It does, with a few adjustments — here's how indoor acoustics, setup, amplification, and coordination with your venue's team differ, so you can plan your ballroom booking with confidence.

## How Indoor Acoustics Differ From Bali's Outdoor Ceremony Spaces

Indoor rooms contain and reflect sound rather than letting it dissipate the way wind, open air, and foliage do at an outdoor Bali ceremony. Walls, a high ceiling, and hard flooring in a ballroom bounce sound back toward your guests instead of it drifting off into a garden or over a cliff edge, so a chamber ensemble's natural acoustic sound often carries further and more evenly indoors than it would outdoors.

The tradeoff is that a very large or mostly empty ballroom can also produce echo or reflections off hard, uncovered surfaces, so where the ensemble sits in the room — not just how loud it plays — does more work indoors than it does outdoors.

## Where a Chamber Ensemble Typically Sets Up in a Ballroom

Musicians typically set up to the side of or just behind where the couple stands, facing the aisle rather than facing guests directly — the same basic placement we use outdoors. What changes indoors is coordinating that spot with the room's existing layout: a stage, dance floor, head table, or AV equipment the resort has already positioned for the day.

Every formation still needs flat, stable floor space for each musician, their instrument, and a stand, and piano — included as standard in Duo, Trio, and String Quartet — needs its own level surface near a power outlet. Walk the room with your venue or planner ahead of time so the musicians' spot is confirmed alongside everything else being set up in the same space.

## Does an Indoor Venue Need Amplification?

Not always. Allegra Chamber Bali doesn't bring its own PA or sound system, and a close-seated indoor ceremony or a smaller reception set often needs none — the room itself contains the sound. A larger ballroom with guests spread across more distance may benefit from light amplification, arranged through your venue's or planner's sound provider rather than by us.

We confirm whether amplification is worth arranging once we know your guest count and the room's layout, as part of the same tech rider process we use for outdoor bookings.

## Coordinating With Resort Event Teams and In-House AV

Many resorts have their own in-house event and AV staff who manage sound, lighting, and staging for the property, though exactly what's available varies from one resort to the next — worth confirming directly with your venue during a walkthrough. Coordination with that team runs through the same point of contact you already have with us, typically you or your wedding planner, rather than a separate line straight to the resort.

That said, when it's the most efficient way to confirm a detail — an outlet location, a staging boundary, a load-in time — we're glad to share our stage plot and tech rider directly with the resort's AV team, with your planner looped in.

## What This Means for Formation and Repertoire Choices

A resort ballroom's fuller, more contained sound suits a fuller formation well: a String Quartet or our Large Ensemble fills a larger reception room without needing amplification in most setups, while a Duo or Trio still works comfortably for a smaller indoor ceremony space. Repertoire choices don't need to change just because you've moved indoors — the same custom arrangements and song requests apply.

| Ballroom Element                     | What It Changes                             | What We Adjust                                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| High ceiling, hard flooring          | Reflects and carries sound further          | Placement and orientation in the room           |
| Large floor, fewer guests spread out | Can produce echo off bare surfaces          | Positioning, sometimes light amplification      |
| Shared or adjoining event spaces     | Needs separation from noise in nearby rooms | Timing and coordination with your resort's team |

None of these rule out live chamber music — they change how we set up for it. Tell us your resort, room, and guest count when you inquire, and we'll confirm formation, placement, and whether amplification is worth arranging.
