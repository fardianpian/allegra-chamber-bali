Picking songs for a string quartet can feel harder than picking the formation itself — do you go
classical, or can your favourite pop song work too? This guide groups the pieces our String
Quartet plays most often by style and by ceremony moment, including how piano changes the sound,
so you can start building a program that fits your day.

## Classical Pieces That Work for String Quartet

Classical repertoire remains the most requested choice for a string quartet, and it reads as
timeless rather than dated years later. From our [repertoire](/repertoire/), a few that come up
often:

- **Canon in D** — Johann Pachelbel: the best-known processional, warm and unhurried.
- **Air on the G String** — J.S. Bach: a slower, more intimate aisle entrance.
- **Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring** — J.S. Bach: a gentle choice for the signing or vow exchange.
- **Ave Maria** — Franz Schubert: a quiet, reflective piece for the ceremony's still moments.
- **Wedding March** — Felix Mendelssohn: bright and triumphant for the recessional.
- **Spring — Allegro** (The Four Seasons) — Antonio Vivaldi: fast and joyful, also for the exit.

All of these work as a quartet alone or with piano joined in, and none need amplification in most
Bali indoor or sheltered outdoor venues.

## Modern and Pop Songs Arranged for Strings

A string quartet isn't limited to classical pieces — pop, film, and contemporary songs are
arranged for strings and piano just as readily, and often read as warmer and more personal for a
ceremony. From our repertoire:

- **Perfect** — Ed Sheeran: a reception favourite that also works earlier in the day.
- **Thinking Out Loud** — Ed Sheeran: a steady, romantic tempo for a first dance or dinner set.
- **A Thousand Years** — Christina Perri: a common pick for the ceremony or reception.
- **Yellow** — Coldplay: an unconventional but increasingly popular processional.
- **Turning Page** — Sleeping at Last: quiet and intimate, suited to the signing.
- **Marry You** — Bruno Mars: upbeat, often chosen for the recessional.

Don't see your song here? Custom arrangements of the couple's own songs are included in every
booking — see the last section below.

## Songs by Moment: Aisle, Signing, and Recessional

Rather than choosing by genre alone, it can help to think moment by moment. Many ceremonies also
open with a few minutes of quiet background music as guests arrive, drawn from the same calmer
repertoire used for cocktail hour.

| Moment                     | Classical pick              | Modern pick                     |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Processional (aisle entry) | Canon in D — Pachelbel      | Yellow — Coldplay               |
| Signing / vow exchange     | Ave Maria — Schubert        | Turning Page — Sleeping at Last |
| Recessional (aisle exit)   | Wedding March — Mendelssohn | Marry You — Bruno Mars          |

A classical and a modern pick for the same moment aren't mutually exclusive — some couples pair a
classical processional with a modern recessional, or the reverse, for a musical contrast across
the ceremony.

## How Piano Changes a String Quartet's Sound

Piano is included as standard with our String Quartet booking — two violins, viola, cello, and
piano, five musicians in total — not a separate add-on. The strings carry the melody while the
piano holds the harmony and rhythm underneath, adding a fuller, rounder sound than strings alone,
especially noticeable on slower pieces like Ave Maria or Canon in D, where the piano gives the
strings something steady to sit on top of.

For a pop or film song, piano also makes the arrangement easier to recognise — it can carry the
song's original chord progression while the strings take the melody or add countermelody lines
around it.

## The Cello's Part: Solo Cello and Cello With Piano

Cello has its own place outside the full quartet, too. It's one of our Solo formation
instruments — violin, cello, or piano, played alone — and it's also one half of our Duo (violin,
cello, and piano). The cello's lower register suits reflective pieces particularly well: Camille
Saint-Saëns wrote **Le Cygne (The Swan)** specifically to showcase a solo cello line over a
rippling piano accompaniment, which is why it's often requested for a cello-and-piano Duo rather
than the full quartet.

If you're drawn to a warmer, lower sound rather than the brighter top line a first violin carries,
a Solo cello or cello-led Duo is worth considering alongside the String Quartet — our
[packages page](/packages/) compares all five formations side by side.

## Requesting a Song That Isn't on the List

The lists above cover what we're asked for most often, not everything we can play. Custom
arrangements of your own song — a pop favourite, a film theme, or something more personal to your
story — are included in every booking, not a separate charge. The earlier you confirm your song
when you inquire, the more time our arrangers have to prepare it properly for strings and piano.
