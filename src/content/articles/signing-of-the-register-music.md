Couples plan the aisle and the exit, then realize they have no idea what should play while signing the register — a quiet pause with no fixed length and no clear cue to end it. Live piano or strings handle that uncertainty naturally, settling into a piece and extending it to match the moment, so you can choose music without worrying about timing it to the second.

## What Happens During the Signing, and How Long It Takes

The signing is a brief, quiet stretch in the ceremony — the couple and witnesses sign the register while the officiant finalises the paperwork, usually lasting two to five minutes. It has no fixed start or stop point the way a processional does, so the music during it works best as a single piece that can be held a little longer or wrapped up early, rather than something timed precisely to a cue.

## Classical Pieces for Signing the Register

A calm, flowing classical piece suits the signing well, since it needs to hold attention quietly without demanding it. Pieces regularly requested for this moment:

| Piece                       | Composer              | Character                                                               |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ave Maria                   | Franz Schubert        | Reverent and flowing — one of the most requested pieces for this moment |
| Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring | Johann Sebastian Bach | Steady and warm, never feels rushed                                     |

Both work well on solo piano or with strings added, and both have the kind of even, unhurried pace that makes them easy to extend by a phrase or two if the signing takes a little longer than expected.

## Why Live Music Handles an Unpredictable Length Better

A recorded playlist either loops awkwardly or cuts off mid-phrase once the signing finishes, because it has no way to respond to what's actually happening at the signing table. Live musicians can do the opposite — holding a phrase a little longer while the paperwork is finished, or settling into a natural stopping point if things wrap up quickly. That same real-time flexibility is also what live music offers through the rest of the ceremony, not just the signing, since the actual pace of a wedding day rarely matches a fixed track exactly.
