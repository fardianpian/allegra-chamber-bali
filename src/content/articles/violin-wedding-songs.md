Many couples want violin in their ceremony but aren't sure which songs suit each moment — a bright entrance piece reads wrong for the signing, and a hushed ballad won't carry down a long aisle. This guide sorts real violin repertoire by moment and formation, so you arrive at your inquiry with a set list in mind.

## Why Violin Carries a Ceremony Melody

Violin projects a clear, singing melodic line even without accompaniment, which is why it's the instrument most couples picture leading a ceremony. Its bright, focused tone carries well outdoors and through a garden or cliffside setting, and it reads as formal or warm depending on the piece chosen. Alone, violin puts the full weight of a piece on one melodic voice; paired with cello or piano, it keeps that same lead line while gaining depth and harmony underneath.

## Classical Violin Pieces for the Aisle

For a traditional entrance, three classical pieces come up most often when couples ask for violin at the aisle:

| Piece               | Composer         | Character                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Air on the G String | J.S. Bach        | Serene and graceful — a classical standard for the walk in |
| Bridal Chorus       | Richard Wagner   | The instantly recognisable entrance from _Lohengrin_       |
| Canon in D          | Johann Pachelbel | Familiar and steady, suited to a measured pace             |

All three suit an unhurried walking pace and work equally well as a violin solo or arranged for Duo, with cello and piano underneath. For a full breakdown of processional order and pacing, our [processional and recessional guide](/journal/wedding-processional-recessional-songs/) covers the walk in more detail.

## Modern Songs Arranged for Violin

Contemporary songs are arranged for violin just as often as classical standards, and the right choice depends on the moment rather than the genre:

| Piece             | Composer/Artist | Ceremony moment | Character                                                |
| ----------------- | --------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Yellow            | Coldplay        | Processional    | A modern entrance that translates beautifully to strings |
| A Whole New World | Alan Menken     | Signing         | A romantic duet suited to the signing of the register    |
| Thinking Out Loud | Ed Sheeran      | Reception       | A first dance favourite, arranged for piano and strings  |
| Perfect           | Ed Sheeran      | Reception       | A global first dance favourite, warm on solo violin      |

A modern song on violin tends to read as more personal than a classical standard, especially when it's tied to your own story rather than chosen for its familiarity.

## Solo Violin or Violin With Piano

Solo violin is a complete, self-sufficient sound — one musician carrying the melody with nothing underneath it, and one of our most requested Solo formation choices. Duo adds cello and piano, included as standard rather than as a paid upgrade: violin and cello carry the melody while piano holds the harmony and rhythm, giving the same lead line a fuller, warmer frame.

Solo violin suits a very intimate ceremony, a small guest list, or simply the sound of a bare, exposed melody. Duo suits a larger or more open venue, or a couple who wants harmony underneath the line rather than the melody alone. ## Choosing Songs by Ceremony Moment

Ceremony moment changes what a violin piece needs to do — carry a slow walk, hold a hushed pause, or send you off at a brighter pace:

| Moment       | Violin song ideas                              | Character                   |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Processional | Air on the G String, Bridal Chorus             | Steady, measured, formal    |
| Signing      | A Whole New World, Ave Maria                   | Hushed and reflective       |
| Recessional  | Wedding March, Spring — Allegro (Four Seasons) | Bright, brief, celebratory  |
| Reception    | Perfect, Thinking Out Loud                     | Warmer, arranged with piano |
