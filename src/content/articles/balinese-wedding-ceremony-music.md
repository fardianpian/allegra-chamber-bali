Many couples marrying in Bali want to honor local Balinese traditions alongside a Western-style ceremony — and the most common question is where live chamber music belongs in that structure. Allegra performs the Western chamber portion; traditional adat elements are coordinated by your planner and local specialists. What follows helps you understand how both parts fit in the same day.

## Why Couples Blend Balinese Elements Into a Destination Wedding

Blending traditions is one of the most common choices for international couples choosing Bali as their wedding destination. Many want the ceremony to reflect the place — not as a theme, but as a genuine engagement with the culture of where their wedding is happening. That might mean an adat blessing, a traditional ritual element, or simply a ceremony setting that reflects Balinese architecture and landscape. Western ceremony music sits alongside this rather than replacing it — the two parts of the day are sequenced, not merged.

## Where Western Ceremony Music Typically Fits Around Adat Elements

Live chamber music most naturally covers the Western ceremony structure: the prelude as guests arrive, the processional when the couple enters, the music during vow exchange or signing, and the recessional. These moments have natural musical bookmarks that a live ensemble follows cue by cue.

Adat elements — which are led by officiants or ritual specialists and follow their own internal timing — typically fall before, after, or in a distinct segment from the Western ceremony portion. Your planner maps where the adat moments sit relative to the processional timeline, so there's no overlap and both parts receive the attention they need.

The ensemble follows the cue from your planner or coordinator. The adat portion is in the hands of those qualified to lead it.

## Working With Your Planner to Sequence Both Traditions

A timeline shared early is the simplest way to avoid gaps or rushes on the day. Your planner typically builds a master ceremony timeline that names each segment and its duration — the processional, any ritual elements, readings, vows, signing, and recessional. Share that timeline with the musicians at least two weeks before the wedding so the set list and transitions can be built around it.

For the Western portions, the musicians follow normal ceremony cues: the planner or coordinator signals the processional start, the musicians read the room for pacing, and the recessional begins on a visible cue from the couple or officiant. This works the same whether or not the ceremony also includes adat elements.

If you're uncertain how much time each segment needs, your planner is the right person to ask — they've worked with blended ceremony structures before and can give realistic estimates.

## What Allegra Provides (and What We Leave to Local Specialists)

Allegra's scope is the Western chamber portion of your ceremony: live piano and strings performing the repertoire you select, from classical pieces to custom arrangements of songs specific to your story. Piano is part of every formation from Duo upward — included as standard, not a separate booking.

What falls outside that scope: traditional Balinese musical elements, adat blessing music, Gamelan, or any other ceremonial music from Balinese or regional Indonesian tradition. These require specialists with the appropriate cultural background, and your wedding planner can connect you with the right people for that portion of the day.

## Questions to Ask When Planning a Blended Ceremony

Before you finalize your ceremony program, these questions help ensure both parts of the day are covered:

- **What adat elements are included, and what is the expected duration of each?** This shapes how much time to allocate and where the Western ceremony music fits around them.
- **Who leads the adat portion?** Confirm they are briefed separately from the Western ceremony musicians.
- **Is the venue setup shared between both parts, or are there separate areas?** Instrument placement may need to work around a space used differently for adat elements.
- **Does the venue or officiant have any requirements around sound during ritual portions?** Some environments call for acoustic-only moments.
- **What is the backup plan if the ceremony runs long?** Your planner and musicians both need the same answer before the day arrives.

Once you have a working ceremony outline, share it with your planner and with us at the same time — it's the fastest way to make sure the two parts of the day are coordinated rather than planned in separate silos.
