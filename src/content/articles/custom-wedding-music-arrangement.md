Most couples know which song they want for their processional long before they understand how it gets from a playlist to a live chamber ensemble. The answer is a custom arrangement — and the process is more straightforward than it might seem once you know what to send and when to ask.

## Why Most Wedding Song Requests Need a Custom Arrangement

Most popular songs were not written for a string ensemble or piano trio. A contemporary pop track, a film score, or a classical piece that exists only as a piano solo all need to be rewritten — voices redistributed across violin, cello, piano, or whichever instruments are in your formation — before a live ensemble can perform it at your ceremony.

This is not a workaround; it is how the piece becomes yours. A custom arrangement means the music is built around your specific formation, your song choices, and the arc of your ceremony rather than adapted from a generic transcription.

## What to Send When You Request a Song

You do not need to supply sheet music. When you share a song request, what helps most is:

- **The song title and artist** — so we know exactly which recording and version you mean.
- **A reference recording** — if you have a specific version in mind (a live recording, a particular cover, a film version with a different tempo), linking to it helps us match the feel you are hearing rather than the most common version.
- **Where in the ceremony you want it** — processional, vow exchange, signing, recessional. Each moment has a different feel and length, which shapes how the arrangement is structured.

That is all. We do not require you to know the key, the time signature, or the instrumentation — those are part of the arrangement itself.

## How the Arrangement Changes by Formation

The same song sounds and functions differently depending on which formation performs it. Knowing your formation before finalising song choices is helpful.

| Formation      | Musicians          | How the arrangement works                                                                        |
| -------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Solo Piano     | 1                  | Piano carries both melody and harmony — arrangement is fully self-contained.                     |
| Duo            | 3 (vln, vc, piano) | Melody in violin, harmony and rhythm in piano, cello adds bass line and countermelody.           |
| Trio           | 4 (+ viola)        | Adds a third string voice — more harmonic depth and richer texture at the same volume.           |
| String Quartet | 5 (+ 2nd vln)      | Two independent string lines above and below; piano grounds the harmony; full four-part voicing. |
| Large Ensemble | 10                 | Full chamber scale — 4 violins, 2 violas, 2 cellos, double bass, and piano.                      |

Piano is part of the standard lineup in every formation from Duo upward — not an add-on. You can read about each formation and what it includes on our [packages page](/packages/).

## How Long a Custom Arrangement Takes, and When to Request It

Confirming your song choices early — ideally when you book — gives the most time for arrangement and any back-and-forth if you want adjustments before the day. The arrangement needs to be completed and rehearsed before the ceremony, which means songs confirmed months ahead have more flexibility than those requested close to the date.

If you are already planning a few months out, the right time to ask is now: let us know your date, your song ideas, and your formation via our [contact page](/contact/), and we can give you a realistic picture of the timeline.

## Can Any Song Be Arranged for a Chamber Ensemble?

Most songs can, and the range is wider than many couples expect — classical pieces, contemporary pop, film and television scores, traditional songs, and original compositions have all been arranged for chamber formations.

Some translate more naturally than others. A melody-forward track with clear harmonic movement is generally easier to arrange than a piece that relies primarily on electronic texture for its effect. The honest answer is: share the song you have in mind when you reach out, and we will tell you directly whether it suits the format and how we would approach it. There are rarely surprises once you tell us what you are picturing.
