You've picked your processional song, but the recessional — the first music you hear as a married couple — is easy to leave for last. The exit moves faster than the entrance and lasts only a minute or two, so it deserves its own thought. Here are classical and modern picks, and how to choose.

## What Is a Wedding Recessional, and How Long Does It Last?

The recessional is the music played as you and your new spouse walk back up the aisle, followed by your bridal party and guests. It usually runs one to two minutes — shorter than the three-to-five-minute processional, since the exit moves at a quicker pace than the entrance. That short runtime is exactly why the piece matters: there's no time to build slowly into it, so it needs to feel right from the first note.

## Classical Recessional Songs

Classical pieces are the traditional choice for a recessional, and they read as timeless rather than dated years later. A few we're regularly asked to arrange for the exit, all available from our [repertoire](/repertoire/):

- **Wedding March** — Felix Mendelssohn: the classic recessional, bright and triumphant from the first bar.
- **Spring — Allegro** (from The Four Seasons) — Antonio Vivaldi: fast, joyful, and celebratory.
- **Eine Kleine Nachtmusik — Allegro** — W.A. Mozart: light and elegant, a gentler exit than the other two.

Any of these works on piano alone or with strings added, and all three carry enough energy on their own that you don't need to pair them with a second piece.

## Upbeat and Modern Recessional Songs, Arranged for Piano and Strings

If classical isn't the tone you want for your exit, a contemporary song arranged for piano and strings reads as warmer and more personal while keeping the brighter tempo a recessional calls for. From our repertoire:

- **L.O.V.E.** — Nat King Cole: a cheerful, contemporary alternative to a classical exit.
- **Marry You** — Bruno Mars: upbeat and high-energy, arranged to carry you straight out the door.
- **Love Story** — Taylor Swift: joyful without losing the ceremony's formality.

Don't see your song on the list? Custom arrangements of the couple's own songs are included in every booking — send it when you inquire and we'll confirm it for your date.

## Wedding Party Recessional: Who Walks Out When

The recessional follows a loose mirror of the processional, reversed: you and your new spouse lead, followed by the flower girl and ring bearer, then the maid of honour and best man, then the bridesmaids and groomsmen in pairs, then parents and grandparents, with guests following after. Smaller Bali ceremonies often simplify this considerably — some skip straight from the couple to guests. Share your actual bridal party size with us and we time the piece to comfortably cover everyone's exit rather than cutting off early.

## Church Wedding Exit Music

For a church or Catholic ceremony, exit music is adapted to that specific church's own guidelines rather than one fixed rule — some churches are flexible on what plays as you leave, others set expectations around it. Classical pieces like Mendelssohn's Wedding March need no adaptation to suit piano and strings, which is part of why they remain a common choice for a church exit. Share your church or officiant's guidelines with us as early as you have them, and we'll confirm the final selection against those rules before the day. ## Timing the Exit: Why Live Musicians Follow Your Pace

A recorded playlist runs on a fixed clock; live musicians don't. If your exit takes a little longer than expected — photos pausing you partway down the aisle, a slower walk than planned — the piece continues and resolves naturally when you reach the end, instead of cutting off mid-phrase or trailing into silence while you're still mid-aisle. That's one of the practical advantages of booking live music specifically for this moment, on top of how the music itself sounds.
