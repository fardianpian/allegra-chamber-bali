A photographer needs to be in position before the processional starts, and a DJ waiting to open the dance floor needs to know exactly when the ensemble's set ends. Live music doesn't run on its own island — it sits inside a shared timeline, so coordinating it in advance keeps every handoff invisible to your couple.

## Why Cross-Vendor Timing Matters More for Live Music Than a Playlist

A DJ-run playlist plays a fixed track for a fixed length, so other vendors can plan around a known duration. Live music doesn't work that way — a piece can stretch or shorten in the moment to match the pace of the walk, which is exactly what makes it feel natural, but it also means the photographer, officiant, and reception team all need the same confirmed cue points, not just a start time on a schedule.

That's why the run-of-show — processional, vow exchange or signing, recessional, and any reception entrances — gets confirmed with us well before the day, then shared across your vendor team so everyone is working from the same reference.

## Working With the Photographer and Videographer

Yes, live music needs to line up with your photography team, and the confirmed run-of-show is what makes that possible. The same cue points our musicians follow — processional start, vow exchange, recessional — let a photographer or videographer be positioned in advance rather than reacting once a moment is already underway.

If a videographer has a specific audio need beyond that, such as requesting a feed from the ceremony sound setup, raise it early so it can be worked out as part of your tech rider rather than assumed on the day. See [Tech Rider Basics for Live Chamber Music at Weddings](/journal/tech-rider-live-wedding-music/) for what that document typically covers.

## Working With the Officiant

The order of the ceremony — when music starts, when it pauses, when it resumes — is confirmed with you in advance, and on the day, our musicians take their go-ahead cue from your planner, coordinator, or officiant rather than working off a fixed clock. Because the performance is live, a piece can extend a few extra bars if the officiant pauses, or wind down early if the walk finishes sooner than expected, instead of a recording that keeps running or cuts out mid-phrase.

That flexibility is only useful if the officiant knows it's there — worth a quick mention in your pre-ceremony briefing so they're comfortable giving a natural cue rather than watching for a track to end.

## Handoff to MC or DJ at Cocktail Hour

The handoff between live music and a DJ or MC is set in the run-of-show before the wedding day, not worked out in real time once the ceremony ends. Live music typically covers the ceremony and, if booked, cocktail hour, then stops at the point the schedule already confirms — [most couples book it this way](/journal/why-planners-recommend-live-music-bali/), with a DJ or band taking over once cocktail hour or dancing begins, rather than the reverse.

Because that boundary is agreed in advance, there's no on-the-day negotiation between the ensemble and the incoming vendor — the schedule does that work before either of them arrives.

## What to Include in Your Vendor Briefing Document

A briefing document that covers live music alongside your other vendors should include:

| Item                                    | Why it matters                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formation and musician count            | Tells your photography and AV teams how much space and setup to plan around  |
| Run-of-show with cue points             | The single reference for musicians, photographers, and the officiant alike   |
| Venue layout and setup location         | Lets other vendors plan sightlines and positioning around where we're set up |
| Handoff point to DJ, MC, or next vendor | Confirms exactly where live music ends and the next part of the day begins   |
| Point of contact for day-of changes     | Keeps a timeline shift from reaching only some of your vendors               |

Sharing this once, well before the wedding day, is what keeps every vendor working from the same version of the schedule.
