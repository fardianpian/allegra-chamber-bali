Couples often plan the ceremony in detail, then realize cocktail hour, dinner, and the first dance all need music too, with no sense of how it fits together. Here is every musical moment of a Bali wedding in order, with how long each typically runs, so you can map a timeline for your planner and musicians.

## Ceremony: Prelude, Processional, Signing, Recessional

The ceremony itself runs through four distinct moments, each with a different job. The prelude plays as guests arrive and are seated, usually two or three pieces; the processional carries the bridal party and couple down the aisle; an optional piece covers the vow exchange or signing, a moment with no fixed length; and the recessional plays as you exit, together as a married couple. Our [guide to how many songs a ceremony needs](/journal/wedding-ceremony-song-count/) breaks down the piece count for each of these, and our [guide to music for signing the register](/journal/signing-of-the-register-music/) covers that moment specifically, since it tends to run the least predictably.

## Cocktail Hour

Cocktail hour shifts from cued moments to one continuous backdrop for guests mingling with a drink in hand. The same musicians booked for your ceremony can typically continue straight into this set without changing formation, drawing on lighter, more conversational repertoire than the processional or recessional. It's booked as its own stretch of coverage rather than an automatic extension — our [cocktail hour music guide](/journal/cocktail-hour-wedding-piano-music/) covers what actually changes once the ceremony ends.

## Reception: Entrance, Dinner, First Dance

The reception brings its own cued moments back into the timeline, starting with any grand entrance — a point confirmed in your run-of-show alongside the ceremony cues, so musicians know exactly when to start. Dinner itself calls for conversational volume rather than a performance guests are asked to watch; any of our formations can play a dinner set by request, continuing straight from cocktail hour or booked as a separate block. The first dance is usually its own moment within that set, and because custom arrangements of your own songs are included in every booking, it's a common one to request as an arrangement for piano and strings rather than the original recording.

## How Long Each Moment Usually Runs

Ceremony moments run to fairly consistent lengths, while the stretches after the ceremony are booked as continuous blocks rather than a fixed number of minutes:

| Moment                          | Typical Duration                               |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Prelude                         | 10–15 min                                      |
| Processional                    | 3–5 min                                        |
| Signing or vow exchange music   | 2–5 min (optional, length varies)              |
| Recessional                     | 1–2 min                                        |
| Cocktail hour                   | Longer continuous block, length set at booking |
| Reception (dinner, first dance) | Length varies with your reception schedule     |

Ceremony timing holds regardless of formation — a solo pianist and a full string quartet both work within the same moment-by-moment structure. What changes with formation and booking length is how much of the day beyond the ceremony gets covered.

## Building Your Timeline With Your Planner and Musicians

Your music timeline comes together the same way the rest of your wedding-day schedule does: as a shared run-of-show, confirmed in advance and worked from by everyone involved, not negotiated moment-to-moment on the day. Musicians take their cue from your planner, coordinator, or officiant at each point rather than a fixed clock, which is part of why live music can stretch a prelude or ease into a recessional without feeling rushed.
